# Endgame Intelligence Specification

Delivery: M7 after the integrated chapter/state/memory loop; depend on
simulation only for modules the novel actually uses. Ending conditions and
resolution decisions are versioned state. Surface their evidence and author
controls; an LLM assessment alone cannot mark a critical promise resolved.

## Ending Vision vs Ending Script

Store durable destination/tone, not a frozen final-chapter script.

Example: - protagonist becomes emperor; - transformed/prosperous
world; - family/relationships receive closure; - legacy established; -
tone triumphant + nostalgic; - main story closed; - after-story
optional.

## Ending conditions

Ending readiness is condition-based: main conflict, protagonist arc,
major relationships, critical promises, major threads, primary
antagonist, world/political state, legacy, and author-defined
conditions.

Target chapter count is soft unless explicitly configured hard.

## Resolution ledger

Classify unresolved items: `MUST_RESOLVE`, `SHOULD_RESOLVE`,
`MAY_RESOLVE`, `INTENTIONALLY_OPEN`, `SEQUEL_HOOK`.

## Endgame mode

As the ending approaches, planning detail increases. After a configured
point of no return, major new villains, systems, mysteries, or love
interests should trigger an endgame-scope warning unless justified.

## Narrative debt burn-down

Critical unresolved debt should trend toward zero. Do not invent new
major debt solely to extend a completed story.

## Callback and legacy

Track callback candidates from early/mid story: places, promises,
people, objects, beliefs, first achievements, former antagonists/allies.

Legacy may include technology, institutions, political reforms, family,
students, culture, historical reputation.

## Epilogue

Support immediate aftermath, later-life epilogue, historical epilogue,
history-vs-truth contrast, and optional after stories.
