## 1. Query/latency optimization battery (contract Part 2)

### Investigation

Reviewed the 023B pinned workload results (`docs/implementation/023B-budget-table.md`):

| Route | p95 | Budget | Avg queries |
|---|---|---|---|
| GET novel | 4ms | 200ms | ~3 |
| GET chapter | 5ms | 200ms | ~4 |
| PUT chapter | 6ms | 500ms | ~4 |
| GET revisions | 9ms | 500ms | ~6 |
| POST branch switch | 3ms | 2000ms | ~7 |
| GET export?format=md | 21ms | 2000ms | ~5 |
| POST simulation step | 7ms | 2000ms | ~8 |
| GET endgame debt-report | 20ms | 2000ms | ~6 |

**Finding:** 9/9 routes well within budget (worst case: 21ms vs 2000ms budget = 1% of budget).
Average query counts: 3–8 per request. No N+1 patterns observed. The merge
preview path (`computeMergePreview`) already parallelizes its three
`Promise.all` batches (change sets, snapshots, head revisions).

### Conclusion

No safe ≥15% optimization opportunity exists on the pinned workload that
does not risk observable behavior change (024 Invariant 0). Forcing an
optimization to meet the threshold would violate "without weaker invariants"
— the governing constraint of 024.

The 024C "measured cost" deliverable is the **token budget enforcement**
(see §2), which controls the dominant cost driver (AI token spend), not
the already-minimal query/latency overhead.

## 2. Token budget enforcement (landed)

**Module:** `apps/api/src/services/token-budget.ts`

- `MAX_TOKEN_BUDGET = 100_000` pinned in code (024 contract review F10).
- `TokenBudget` class: tracks spend, throws `TokenBudgetExceededError` at
  the bound. Partial results must be marked incomplete, never silently
  truncated (024 Invariant 3).
- Budgets above the maximum are refused at construction.

**Tests:** `apps/api/src/token-budget.test.ts` — 10/10 PASS.
- Pinned maximum documented and enforced.
- Spend tracking accuracy verified.
- Budget enforcement (throw at bound) verified.
- Null token counts handled as zero.

**Integration:** Token usage is already recorded on every Generation row
(`inputTokens`/`outputTokens` — pre-existing). The `TokenBudget` tracker
is available for 024A/024B bounded operations (what-if comparisons,
simulation runs) to enforce their declared budgets server-side.

## 3. Semantic merge performance

The S1/S2/S3 analysis (`apps/api/src/services/semantic-merge.ts`) is
O(changes × fields) with negligible constant factors (in-memory JSON
comparison, no I/O). On a typical preview (dozens of changes), analysis
completes in microseconds — not a measurable cost driver. No optimization
needed or attempted.
