# Narrative DNA & Planning

Integration gate: approved outline and consumed DNA fields must reach the
shared chapter compiler and final-text review, with source versions pinned.
Stored JSON or pure rules alone do not prove planning behavior. Validate each
consumed policy field; reject unsupported values instead of silently storing
them as effective configuration. Revalidate output when the source plan changes.

## Narrative DNA

Narrative DNA stores durable author preferences describing how the novel
should feel. Fields should be extensible and mostly policy-driven.

Examples: - genre/subgenre tags; - progression pace; - protagonist
competence; - drama density; - typical drama gap and occasional extended
gap; - comedy/tragedy tolerance; - romance structure; - face-slapping
frequency; - enemy-to-ally preference; - political complexity; -
technology realism; - war frequency; - ending tone; - target length and
whether it is hard/soft.

Narrative DNA is not an outline. Values are soft constraints unless
explicitly marked hard.

## Planning hierarchy

Novel Vision -\> Saga -\> Major Arc -\> Mini Arc -\> Chapter Group -\>
Chapter -\> Scene.

## Planning Horizon

Detail decreases with narrative distance. Example only: - next 5--10
chapters: very detailed; - next 20--50: detailed; - next 100--250:
arc-level; - distant sagas: direction; - ending: vision/conditions.

Do not encode those ranges as universal constants.

## Synopsis anti-overfitting

Synopsis is primarily used for premise, genre, central conflict, broad
direction, initial macro plot and initial outline.

After approved detailed planning exists: - do not restate synopsis as
chapter prose; - do not force every chapter toward a synopsis bullet; -
do not rush distant synopsis events; - do not use synopsis wording as a
substitute for scene planning.

Chapter decisions come from approved outline + accumulated state +
causality + continuity.
