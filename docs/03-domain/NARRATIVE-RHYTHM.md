## Principle

Rhythm preferences are soft constraints. Never implement "drama every N
chapters" as a cron-like rule.

Integration must honor the configured hardness of each consumed concern.
An unconditional hard refusal is valid only for an explicit hard invariant
or authorized hard policy; soft preferences remain evidence-backed advice.
Derive story measurements from revision-linked events where available. A
caller-supplied estimate must be labeled as such, not treated as observed
chapter history. The author UI must expose policy and warning evidence.

## Drama policy

Example author preference: - density: low; - typical gap: \~30--50
chapters; - occasional extended calm: \~70--140; - strict interval:
false.

Planner considers chapters since meaningful drama, current arc, tension,
threads, recent payoffs, upcoming payoffs, story scale, repetition risk,
and author instruction.

## Drama types

Social, personal, romance, political, economic, scholarly,
military/conflict, family, ideological, or custom.

## Temporary antagonists

Roles include MAIN, ARC, TEMPORARY, RIVAL, IDEOLOGICAL, POLITICAL,
PERSONAL, COMPETITOR.

Temporary antagonists may exist for \~5--20 chapters when appropriate.
Their purpose must be explicit.

Lifecycle: introduction -\> motivation -\> friction -\>
underestimation/conflict -\> escalation -\> confrontation -\>
defeat/realization -\> consequence -\> resolution.

Possible outcomes: regret, resentment, retreat, loss of status, exile,
death, neutrality, respect, redemption, alliance, subordination, future
revenge.

## Enemy-to-ally

A serious character normally requires evidence across stages: hostile
-\> defeated -\> acknowledgement -\> reluctant respect -\> observation
-\> cooperation -\> trust -\> ally.

## Underestimation

Require a plausible reason: status, age, outdated information,
prejudice, false intelligence, overconfidence, cultural bias, etc.
Cross-check reputation/information reach.

## Pattern detection

Detect repeated sequences such as: underestimate -\> provoke -\> MC
demonstrates -\> public humiliation.

Recommend variation rather than banning the trope.

## Scale appropriateness

Narrative scale evolves: personal -\> family -\> village -\> town -\>
city -\> province -\> kingdom -\> empire.

Conflict proposals should be checked against current protagonist status,
access, reputation, and scale.
