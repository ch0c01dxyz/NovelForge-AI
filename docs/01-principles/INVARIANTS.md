# Core Invariants

## Truth and memory

-   Canonical relational state is authoritative.
-   Embeddings, summaries, caches, LLM memory, and generated candidate
    facts are rebuildable projections.
-   Published prose may be evidence for canon extraction but does not
    bypass validation policy.

## Planning

-   Do not detail-plan thousands of chapters.
-   Near-term plans are detailed; distant plans are progressively
    abstract.
-   Synopsis cannot mechanically override accumulated story state.
-   Significant outline deviation is visible and traceable.

## Character and relationships

-   Genius does not mean omniscient.
-   Characters may only act on knowledge they possess/suspect or can
    reasonably infer.
-   Relationship structures are graph-based, not limited to one
    love-interest field.
-   Marriage is a state transition, not the end of character
    development.
-   Harem/polygamy configuration never implies every compatible
    character must become romantic.

## Simulation

-   Knowledge of a technology does not imply capability to produce it.
-   Progress has prerequisites, diffusion, stakeholders, costs, and
    consequences.
-   Opponents can learn from observed strategies.
-   Historical future knowledge becomes less reliable as divergence
    increases.
-   Simulation modules are optional and composed from generic
    primitives.

## Narrative rhythm

-   Drama intervals are preferences/windows, not fixed timers.
-   Repeated conflict/payoff patterns are detected.
-   Conflict scale should evolve with protagonist status and world
    position.
-   Temporary antagonists have explicit narrative purpose and lifecycle.

## Ending

-   Chapter count is normally a soft target.
-   Ending readiness depends on resolution conditions, not a number
    alone.
-   Not every minor thread must resolve; resolution policy distinguishes
    MUST/SHOULD/MAY/OPEN/SEQUEL.
-   The planner must not invent major narrative debt merely to extend a
    story whose ending conditions are satisfied.

## Recovery

-   No destructive rollback.
-   High-impact changes require impact analysis and pre-change
    checkpoint.
-   Restore creates a new revision.
-   Selective restore requires dependency/conflict analysis.
